PART A - PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY (read this first)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE PROBLEM HE WORKS ON

When a wing moves gently through steady air, predicting lift is a solved
problem - you can do it with equations from the 1930s. But when a wing pitches
sharply, flaps, changes shape, or flies into a gust, the air separates off the
front edge and rolls up into a vortex. That vortex sits on top of the wing and
changes the forces dramatically, sometimes doubling the lift for a moment, then
dumping it when the vortex washes away. This is what insects, birds and bats
exploit to fly, and it is what makes small drones, helicopter rotors and wind
turbine blades hard to design.

The honest way to predict this is full CFD, which takes hours or days per run.
That is far too slow to use inside a design loop where you want to try
thousands of wing shapes or motions. So the field needs something in between:
fast enough to run in a loop, accurate enough to trust.

WHAT HE BUILT

Ramesh's career contribution is exactly that middle tool. The headline idea is
a single number he calls the Leading-Edge Suction Parameter (LESP). It measures
how hard the air is being pulled around the front edge of the wing. The insight
is that when LESP crosses a critical threshold, a vortex starts forming - and
below it, the flow stays attached. That one number converts a messy, hard-to-
predict event into a simple on/off criterion you can check at every time step.

He wrapped that criterion into a method (LDVM) that represents the wing and its
wake as a cloud of discrete vortex points, releasing a new one whenever LESP
goes critical. The result runs in tens of seconds instead of hours, and matches
CFD and wind-tunnel experiments well.

WHERE HE IS NOW

Two recent directions:

  1. MORPHING WINGS (2024). Instead of just accepting whatever vortex the wing
     produces, can you change the wing's shape mid-flight to control the vortex
     on purpose - make it form earlier, later, stronger, weaker? His student's
     work extended the model to wings that bend substantially, which the
     classical theory could not handle. This is a design/control question, which
     is why it is the most relevant thread for our class.

  2. GETTING THE MATH RIGHT (2025). A technical correction to how the shed wake
     is represented at each time step, which makes the model both more
     rigorous and cheaper. Signals that he cares about correctness, not just
     speed.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR OUR PROJECT

The thing to understand before the interview: he already has a fast surrogate.
We cannot walk in and offer him speed. Anything we propose has to address
either accuracy where his model is currently wrong, or a search problem his
fast model still cannot make tractable.

PART B - PAPER 1 (FOUNDATION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ramesh, K., Gopalarathnam, A., Granlund, K., Ol, M.V. & Edwards, J.R. (2014)
"Discrete-vortex method with novel shedding criterion for unsteady aerofoil
flows with intermittent leading-edge vortex shedding."
Journal of Fluid Mechanics, vol. 751, pp. 500-538.

WHAT PROBLEM IT SOLVES
Existing low-order methods either assumed vortices shed continuously (only
valid for sharp leading edges) or used ad-hoc criteria like a fixed critical
angle of attack (only valid for a narrow set of motions). Classical unsteady
airfoil theories were limited to small amplitudes and attached leading-edge
flow. Neither covered the realistic case: a rounded leading edge with vortices
forming and stopping intermittently.

KEY TECHNICAL POINTS
  - Introduces the Leading-Edge Suction Parameter (LESP) as a criterion for
    intermittent shedding from rounded leading edges, governed by a maximum
    allowable leading-edge suction.
  - Shows that within unsteady thin-airfoil theory, LESP is the A0 term of the
    Fourier series representing the chordwise variation of bound vorticity.
    (This is the elegant part - the criterion is not bolted on, it falls out of
    the existing theory.)
  - When instantaneous LESP exceeds the critical value, a discrete vortex is
    released at the leading edge, with strength set so LESP is held AT the
    critical value during shedding.
  - A single empirical parameter (critical LESP) determines onset, growth and
    termination of the leading-edge vortex.
  - Validated against CFD and experiment across motions from the unsteady
    airfoil literature.

THE OPEN SOFT SPOT
Critical LESP is empirical. A companion 2012 AIAA paper (Ramesh, Ke,
Gopalarathnam & Edwards) showed it depends on airfoil shape and Reynolds
number - increasing leading-edge radius raises the critical value, because a
more rounded edge supports more suction before separating. They tabulated
values across a parameter range. CONFIRM IN THE INTERVIEW whether this is
still table lookup from CFD.

RELATED FOLLOW-ON
SureshBabu, Ramesh & Gopalarathnam (2019), AIAA Journal 57(4), 1409-1422,
"Model reduction in discrete-vortex methods for unsteady airfoil flows."
Merges pairs of discrete vortices under a condition that preserves the velocity
at the leading edge, conserving bound circulation and leading-edge suction.
Same accuracy, significantly lower runtime. Relevant because it shows he has
ALREADY optimized his own method's cost.

PART C - PAPER 2 (CURRENT STUDENT LINE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Martinez-Carmena, A. & Ramesh, K. (2024)
"Inviscid modeling of unsteady morphing airfoils using a discrete-vortex
method."
Theoretical and Computational Fluid Dynamics, vol. 38(6), pp. 845-862.
DOI: 10.1007/s00162-023-00678-7   (open access)
Funded by EPSRC.

WHAT PROBLEM IT SOLVES
Thin-airfoil theory assumes small flow disturbances, which breaks as soon as
the wing itself deforms substantially. Morphing wings - variable camber,
trailing-edge flaps, anything bio-inspired that bends - fall outside that
assumption. This paper extends the low-order model to large-amplitude camber
change.

KEY TECHNICAL POINTS
  - Combines potential-flow theory adapted for unsteady airfoils with discrete-
    vortex numerical methods, to get rapid predictions of flow behavior and
    force evolution.
  - Core trick: a TIME-VARYING CHORD LINE over which the boundary condition is
    satisfied, rather than a fixed one. This is what escapes thin-airfoil
    theory's small-disturbance restriction and allows large camber-line
    deformation.
  - The difference from the fixed-chord formulation is most pronounced for
    large-amplitude deformations.
  - Keywords the authors list: morphing airfoil, variable camber, trailing-edge
    flap, vortex methods, thin-airfoil theory, bio-inspired.

THE DESIGN ANGLE (why this is our best thread)
The associated PhD thesis - Martinez Carmena, "Vortex flows around morphing
foils: reshape yourself to effect a change," University of Glasgow 2023,
supervised by Ramesh - states the goal directly: demonstrate that camber
morphing can affect leading-edge vortices, with the amount of vorticity
produced at the leading edge modulated by suitably tailoring the shape of the
camber line. The thesis also notes the strength of nascent particles at the
leading edge is computed from the velocity at the edge of the shear layer,
formulated in terms of LESP.

In other words: shape parameters in, vortex characteristics out. That is a
parametric design map, which is a many-query problem, which is what our class
is about. ASK HIM HOW HE SEARCHES THAT SPACE.

PART D - PAPER 3 (NEWEST, ASU-AFFILIATED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ramesh, K. (2025)
"Correct Wake Vorticity Shedding in Time-Stepping Unsteady Thin-Airfoil Theory."
AIAA Journal. DOI: 10.2514/1.J065056
Sole-authored. Listed under Arizona State University, Tempe.

NOTE ON SOURCING: this paper is paywalled and we have only the title,
affiliation and subject tags. The content below is from its free precursor
preprint, arXiv:2205.08647, "On Satisfying the Kutta Condition in Unsteady Thin
Aerofoil Theory" (May 2022), which covers the same contribution. Treat as
closely related, not identical.

WHAT PROBLEM IT SOLVES
In unsteady thin-airfoil theory you must apply a Kutta condition at the
trailing edge to pin down the circulation around the airfoil. How that is done
in a time-stepping scheme turns out to matter, and the conventional treatment
is not strictly correct.

KEY TECHNICAL POINTS
  - Conventional approach represents the wake shed at each discrete time step
    as a point vortex or a regularized vortex blob.
  - His approach instead represents it as a CONTINUOUS DISTRIBUTION OF
    VORTICITY derived from the exact Wagner solution.
  - Demonstrated on three canonical cases: step change in angle of attack
    (Wagner problem), harmonic heaving (Theodorsen problem), and a
    pitch-ramp-hold maneuver.
  - Verified against exact analytical solutions AND CFD of the incompressible
    Euler equations.
  - Result: Kutta condition satisfied at all reduced frequencies, with finite
    velocities and pressure difference going to zero at the trailing edge.
  - Claims improvement on three axes simultaneously - theoretical rigor,
    computational cost, and numerical accuracy.

WHAT IT TELLS US ABOUT HIM
He is actively working on correctness of low-order methods, not just their
speed. A pitch framed as "we made it faster" will land badly. A pitch framed as
"we found where it is wrong" is the better frame.

USEFUL SIDE BENEFIT FOR OUR PROJECT
The Wagner and Theodorsen problems have EXACT ANALYTICAL SOLUTIONS. If we ever
need a bulletproof reference solution (rubric item P1), those are free.

PART E - THE THROUGH-LINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Across all three papers the pattern is the same:

  START    classical potential-flow theory (Wagner, Theodorsen, thin-airfoil)
  ADD      discrete-vortex elements to capture shed vorticity
  FIX      the one place the classical theory breaks
             2014 - it cannot handle intermittent leading-edge shedding  -> LESP
             2024 - it cannot handle large shape change  -> time-varying chord
             2025 - it mishandles the wake at the trailing edge  -> Wagner-based
                    continuous vorticity distribution
  VALIDATE against CFD and experiment
  GOAL     a tool fast and rigorous enough for design and control loops

He is not a CFD person and he is not an ML person. He is a theory-plus-low-
order-modeling person whose stated ambition at ASU includes artificial
intelligence but who has, as far as we can find, no published ML work.
